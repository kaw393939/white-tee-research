# Research brief

## Objective

Understand how focused clothing brands compete, using plain white T-shirts as our product lens. Build a credible competitor landscape that can inform a future brand. The current phase is information collection.

## Scope

- **Product:** plain white T-shirts. Record neckline, fit, intended wearer, and whether the item is an undershirt or outerwear.
- **Primary lens:** brands built around one product or a narrow hero-product proposition.
- **Comparison unit:** one named white T-shirt SKU, variant, and pack size at a time.
- **Market and audience:** undecided. Always record sales region, currency, and intended customer rather than assuming comparability.
- **Later work:** brand identity, sourcing, product design, unit economics, and launch planning follow research.

## Competitor groups

| Group | Working definition | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Single-product brand | Current assortment is centered on one product type; variations do not automatically imply a broad range | Closest business-model reference |
| T-shirt specialist | T-shirts are central but the assortment may include other products | Direct product and positioning comparison |
| Broader benchmark | A wider apparel brand with a relevant white T-shirt | Price, quality claims, distribution, or customer expectation benchmark |

Check the current catalog before assigning a group. A brand that launched with one product may have expanded. Record origin story separately from current assortment.

## Questions to answer

1. What promise makes a seemingly basic product feel distinctive?
2. Which customers and wearing occasions does each brand serve?
3. How do fabric, opacity, fit, neck construction, and durability claims differ?
4. What are the comparable single-shirt and per-shirt pack prices?
5. How do shipping, returns, channels, and availability affect the offer?
6. What do customers repeatedly value or criticize, and how strong is that evidence?
7. Which potential gaps deserve customer interviews or physical product testing?

## Initial research target

Working target: 8–12 qualified profiles across the three groups. This is a planning target, not a finding. Validate the market and audience before narrowing the final sample.
