# Research method

## Collection workflow

1. Discover a candidate and record the official website.
2. Check its current assortment and qualify its competitor group.
3. Select an in-stock or explicitly unavailable plain white T-shirt SKU.
4. Copy the [profile template](/competitors/template) to a new Markdown file.
5. Collect product, price, policy, positioning, and customer evidence.
6. Add source IDs to the [source log](/research/sources), then cite them beside claims.
7. Update the [directory](/competitors/) and [comparison matrix](/research/comparison).
8. Review gaps and propose hypotheses separately from observed facts.

## Evidence rules

- Prefer official product and policy pages for specifications, prices, assortment, and terms.
- A brand claim is not an independently verified performance result. Label it as a claim.
- Use independent reviews for customer signals; record sample size, selection method, dates, and incentives when known.
- Every factual finding needs a source URL, access date, and relevant region or variant.
- Mark unknowns **Not researched**, **Not disclosed**, or **Not applicable**. Never use zero to mean missing.
- Preserve conflicting evidence and explain the disagreement.
- Refresh changing facts before using them to make a decision.

## Price normalization

Record currency, region, tax treatment, regular price, observed sale price, pack quantity, and shipping separately. Calculate per-shirt price as pack price divided by shirt count. Do not compare unlike currencies without recording the exchange rate, source, and conversion date. Do not treat a temporary discount as regular pricing.

## Status and confidence

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Candidate | Discovered; relevance not checked |
| Qualified | Current assortment and relevant product checked |
| In research | Profile partially populated |
| Reviewed | Core fields sourced and gaps explicitly documented |
| Refresh needed | Evidence is stale or contradictory |

Confidence applies to each claim: **high** for clear, directly supported facts; **medium** for partial or indirect support; **low** for tentative interpretations. Confidence is not a brand quality score.
